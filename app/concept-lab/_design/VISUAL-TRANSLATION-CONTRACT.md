# Visual Translation Contract

This contract governs the design layer. It does not replace canonical
academic content, provenance, sources or record types.

1. Canonical content owns scholarly truth: claims, definitions, evidence,
   qualifications, limitations, citations and sources remain live HTML.
2. The experience manifest describes visual interpretation and page behaviour,
   not scholarly truth.
3. The art manifest describes authored visual material, its possible roles and
   its accessibility context.
4. A persuasive image does not become evidence. Authored and generated scenes
   are teaching or editorial material unless a source is explicitly identified.
5. Code owns state, comparison, timing, responsive composition and accessible
   conceptual change. It does not recreate expressive materiality by default.
6. **SAME HAND · DIFFERENT MIND:** mature experiences share type, white canvas,
   material language, provenance, accessibility and quiet scholarship while
   discovering their own geometry from the knowledge.
7. **THE PAGE REMEMBERS THINKING:** circle, fade, residue, rewrite, boundary,
   margin note, repeated crop and question mark are verbs for future visual
   translation. They are not a global decoration kit.
8. Begin a new page by asking: **What shape is this knowledge?**

The reader must be able to understand the academic argument without relying on
raster handwriting. When an authored source field is reused, the consuming
context decides whether it is decorative, contextual or explanatory; one
asset does not have one permanent alt string.
