# Visual quality review

Review the completed page in a browser. Use screenshots of the actual rendered page and its meaningful interaction states. A successful build, passing automated checks, or a plausible diagram does not establish visual quality.

Compare the screenshots with the reference boards that informed the brief and with the relevant approved experience. Review the full images and the rendered page. Do not treat reference boards or approved experiences as page templates.

## Artistic identity

- Does the page keep a clean white editorial field?
- Do typography and drawing feel like one authored explanation?
- Does material choice show expressive care, with visible pressure, pigment, layering, or texture where useful?
- Is the pigment range rich enough for this knowledge, and do the colours work together?
- Does the page avoid a small fixed accent palette when the knowledge calls for a broader range?
- Does each annotation, circle, arrow, boundary, or residual mark do intellectual work?
- Is authored artwork clear and specific to the concept? Inspect procedural diagrams at their actual size; do not assume code-drawn marks look hand-made or conceptually clear.
- Do marks have enough contrast and a clear relationship to labels and live text?

## Composition and scholarship

- Does the opening help a reader recognise the knowledge object's central problem?
- Does composition follow the knowledge rather than a copied page sequence?
- Do scale, contrast, grouping, whitespace, and changes in density support the reading?
- Does the page have a useful visual rhythm, including quiet places for scholarship?
- Do live academic text, artwork, and interaction work as one explanation?
- Can a reader understand the argument without relying on handwritten text or a raster image?
- Are source claims, findings, teaching constructions, editorial readings, uncertainty, citations, and limitations distinct?
- Could any colour, line, position, arrow, size, or animation imply unsupported causality, magnitude, or certainty?
- Does provenance say what each mark or image represents and what it does not establish?

## Interaction and access

- Does each control help a reader understand a concept, relation, comparison, or state?
- Does the page retain a useful static reading path?
- Can a reader operate each control by keyboard and touch, with visible focus and clear state?
- Is essential information available without hover, colour, or motion?
- Does reduced motion preserve the complete explanation?
- Does the mobile composition make the same academic relationships legible without shrinking a dense desktop layout?

## Compare the rendered result

Capture the initial reading view and each meaningful interactive state at a desktop width and a narrow mobile width. Include a very narrow viewport when diagrams or long labels need review. Check text and annotation legibility at the size readers encounter them.

For each relevant reference board, ask what material, mark quality, colour relation, scale, or reading rhythm the page learned from it. Then ask how the record's knowledge changed the composition. The page should belong to the shared artistic world and still have its own geometry.

Use approved experiences for the design problem they document. The Home and Library are protected experiences; do not change their art, content, composition, styling, or functionality as part of this work. Do not copy either one as a general record template.

## Review record

Write down:

- The route and state reviewed.
- The desktop and mobile screenshots reviewed.
- Which reference board and approved experience informed the review.
- What supports conceptual clarity and artistic identity.
- Any issues with authored artwork, typography, whitespace, mobile composition, accessibility, accuracy, or provenance.
- Whether the page is ready for presentation, and which concrete changes remain.

A visual review needs an editorial judgment with screenshot evidence. Automated results can support that judgment, but they cannot replace it.
