# Record creative brief

Complete this brief after reading the relevant source and before choosing page sections, artwork, or interaction. Use the source record and its evidence as the starting point. Do not copy the nearest page layout.

If the knowledge needs no authored artwork or interaction, say why. A clear typographic explanation can be the right design.

## Intellectual problem

- What question or difficulty does the knowledge address?
- What does a reader commonly misunderstand?
- Which distinction is hardest to keep in view?
- What does the source establish, and where does the claim stop?
- What should the reader be able to explain, compare, trace, or judge afterward?

## Visual idea

- What does the reader most need to see?
- What geometry follows from the knowledge: spatial, temporal, causal, comparative, hierarchical, conditional, recursive, or another form?
- What visual metaphor could help? What false implication might it create?
- Which relevant reference boards informed the choice?
- What is the page's principal composition? Describe the field, scale, contrast, whitespace, and reading rhythm without copying a board.
- Which visual elements are authored artwork, live HTML, or interface code?

## Reading and interaction

- What remains visible while the reader explores?
- What changes, and what should the reader understand from that change?
- Which controls, if any, make a useful distinction easier to understand?
- How will the same idea remain understandable without interaction or motion?
- How will the layout recompose on a narrow screen?
- What needs a label, caption, text alternative, visible focus, keyboard path, or touch target?

## Academic and provenance boundaries

- Which claims, measures, citations, and limitations must stay explicit?
- Which marks or images are teaching constructions rather than evidence?
- How will the page distinguish source claims from paraphrase, platform explanation, editorial synthesis, critique, and open questions?
- Could position, line weight, colour, overlap, or animation imply more than the sources establish?
- Which record relations are defensible, and which remain proposed?

## Design decision

Summarise the learning problem, the chosen visual idea, and why the reader should benefit from it. Record alternatives you considered and the reason you set them aside. Name any question that still needs source reading or a design prototype.

Do not turn this brief into a universal section order. Its job is to connect the knowledge to a page-specific composition, while keeping evidence, accessibility, and reader purpose explicit.
