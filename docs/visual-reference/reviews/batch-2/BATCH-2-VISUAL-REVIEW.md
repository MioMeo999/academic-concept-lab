## Proposed group

Batch 2 pairs three music-expectation records whose shared subject leads to
different academic objects and different ways to learn:

- **Meyer’s Expectancy Theory** asks how style-grounded implication, tendency,
  delay and later musical context shape meaning in a historical
  aesthetic-psychological account.
- **Huron’s ITPRA Theory** distinguishes five proposed response functions
  around anticipated outcomes. Its teaching field uses the outcome as a hinge,
  rather than implying five equal steps or a measured physiological trace.
- **IDyOM** is a computational model that assigns probabilities across
  possible musical events and derives information measures. The page keeps
  those model quantities separate from a listener’s measured experience and
  from any claim about a literal neural mechanism.

## Experience decisions

### Meyer — an unfinished phrase, heard again

The opening field suspends one event and lets three qualitative continuations
diverge from the same hinge. A pale backward trace carries the eye back toward
the earlier event, introducing Meyer’s retrospective account without turning
the marks into probability or affect scales. The existing constructed audio
choices remain the reader’s way to hear a strongly implied, plausible or less
implied continuation. Starting a choice marks its endpoint in the field and
announces the qualitative reading in live text, so the figure, sound and
explanation share one interaction state. Later sections preserve fulfilment,
delay, diversion and the hypothetical/evident/determinate distinction.

The composition draws on the reference boards’ layered pencil-and-pigment
marks, visible hand pressure and annotated white space. Its branching geometry
comes from alternative musical futures, not from the predictive-processing
board’s network layout.

### Huron — the outcome as a temporal hinge

A wide, materially varied stroke field narrows at one central event and opens
again after it. The timing interaction immediately below holds the outcome
constant while the reader changes its arrival; the later response-lens
interaction changes the question asked of the same surprise. Prediction and
Reaction remain distinct and parallel in the page’s timeline, while Appraisal
keeps a longer, revisable reach.

The closeout gives each response name, function, question and boundary enough
horizontal space to stay readable beside its plotted band. Prediction and
Reaction keep the same post-onset span. The event hinge and the other three
windows keep their prior positions. The timing comparison now labels expected
and actual arrivals on separate rows and shows the unchanged values in three
readout fields. It remains a qualitative teaching example, not a clock or
physiological measure.

The broad composition uses the boards’ expressive stroke vocabulary and the
shared editorial rhythm. Its central axis is a teaching coordinate only: it
does not encode durations, neural activity or physiological magnitude.

### IDyOM — context before a distribution

The hero layers longer learned musical contours with a shorter local phrase,
then leaves candidate events visibly separate. The actual probability
distribution is rendered in live HTML instead of being implied by the artwork.
The calculated comparison now follows the opening explanation, so readers can
move directly from context and candidate events to the distribution and the
difference between entropy and event information content. The existing early
/ later current-piece interaction remains available later in the account.

The layered field and limited chromatic marks echo the material and pigment
boards. The page keeps model context, numerical outputs and brain claims in
separate representational spaces.
