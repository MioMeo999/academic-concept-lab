# Academic Concept Lab — Page Behaviour

This describes how the visual language behaves across the site. It does not prescribe one template.

## Reference library

Use `docs/visual-language/references/README.md` as a study shelf before a
major visual composition. Inspect only the groups relevant to the question at
hand, then translate their material vocabulary into a geometry discovered from
the content. The references are not production assets or layout presets, and
they do not require a page to contain artwork.

## Home

The Home page should establish the world of Academic Concept Lab before explaining its architecture. It should feel expansive, intelligent, inviting, connected, visually alive and more atlas than landing page.

The opening should combine strong editorial typography, one substantial conceptual drawing, visible human thinking and a clear way into the atlas. Avoid a generic hero illustration beside marketing copy.

Suggested rhythm:

**strong opening → discipline exploration → record-kind explanation → selected entry points → trust/provenance → quiet ending**

At the product level, the concise orientation is **HOME → orient · LIBRARY →
discover · SAVED → return**. This describes different entry purposes, not a
mandatory linear journey; users may enter any surface directly.

## Explore by discipline

Discipline sections should feel like intellectual territories, not generic cards.

Organisational Behaviour might use people, systems, groups, structures, organisational layers, person-environment relation and networks.

Music Psychology might use perception, sound, time, grouping, attention, cognition, musical structure, brain/body/listening and notation fragments where relevant.

The disciplines should feel related but not identical.

## Four record kinds

The project distinguishes Theory, Mechanism, Method and Study. They should feel like different forms of knowledge, not four coloured menu cards.

- **Theory** — lens, frame or structure for understanding
- **Mechanism** — process, pathway or transformation
- **Method** — practice, operation or way of producing/analysing evidence
- **Study** — argument built from a specific design, sample, measure, result and limitation

## Start Here

Selected starting records should feel curated. Show what kind of record each is, what question it helps answer, why it is a useful entry point, and one small record-specific visual cue.

Keep this visually lighter than the opening.

## Saved / personal return surface

Saved is a personal working set for returning to knowledge. Keep it simpler
than the shared Library and prioritise recognition, a direct return action,
reversible removal and truthful device-local persistence. Design deliberately
for zero, one and many items; add grouping only when it helps a larger,
diverse set recognise what it kept. Do not invent recency, organisation tools
or discovery controls that the data model does not support.

## About / epistemic transparency

About is a reader-facing explanation of how the Lab represents knowledge:
**SHOW HOW WE KNOW**. It should make visible what kind of knowledge object the
reader is encountering, how source literature becomes a record, how claims
retain provenance, where visual explanation stops and empirical support begins,
and why uncertainty remains a legitimate state. Its geometry should follow the
role of each meta-content passage: classification, transformation and trail,
claim legend, explanatory/evidential boundary, uncertainty pause and reader
orientation. Keep the canvas reflective and primarily static; live HTML,
typography, paper/source fragments, graphite traces and marginalia can create
an authored research atmosphere without requiring major artwork. About is a
benchmark role, not a reusable composition.

## Theory pages

Theory pages may differ strongly in composition. The theory determines where drawing dominates, where comparison appears, where uncertainty becomes visible, where annotations belong, and where the page becomes quiet.

Avoid the same hero arrangement for every theory.

When a theory already contains a useful comparison, toggle, contextual change,
reclassification or relationship trace, preserve it as part of the visual
translation. `INTERACTION-GRAMMAR.md` defines **Interactive Editorial Drawing**:
live HTML, theory-specific interaction and authored drawing should operate as
one explanatory system. Identify what stays constant and what changes before
choosing artwork or controls. Focus may strengthen one state while leaving a
residual trace of another when the theory says they coexist.

### Affective Events Theory benchmark

AET is temporal and episodic, so its page geometry follows moments,
interruptions, residue, within-person change and later evaluation rather than
generalising a reusable storyboard. Its workplace illustration carries the
lived environment, human presence, gesture, event, reaction and continuity;
canonical claims remain live HTML.

When an expressive illustration teaches the experience better than a complete
formal diagram, let the artwork explain the experience and keep structural
precision in a live ledger. Time can be expressed through sequence,
timestamps, changing crops, residue, transitions and later scenes rather than
as a labelled causal node. On narrow screens, **mobile may re-sequence the
visual argument without changing the theory**: environment → event → reaction
→ affect-driven response → evaluation → deliberate action.

Use **FOCUS WITHOUT DISAPPEARANCE** for comparative states when both routes
remain theoretically present. A macrostructure may then support **SAME MAP ·
DEEPER READING**: the second state interrogates what happens between the same
relationships without replacing the organising theory or implying that later
mechanisms were fully specified by it.

## Evidence

Evidence should feel like a research ledger, not colourful cards. Preserve source, design, what was tested, what was found, what was not tested, limitations and source responsibility.

Use restrained rules, source braces, careful underlines, small provenance marks, light evidence traces and marginal caution notes.

## Scope

Scope should make boundaries visible: inside/outside, partial edge, fading extent, labelled stopping point, nearby-but-excluded ideas.

The visual should distinguish what a theory explains, where it stops and what would be an overclaim.

## Limitations

Use more white space, sparse marks, questions, interrupted lines, partial erasure, caution notes and open boundaries. Avoid dramatic warning design.

## Provenance

Canonical provenance remains:

- `●` source-grounded
- `■` empirical / faithful explanation
- `▲` constructed teaching
- `✦` Concept Lab synthesis/editorial
- `?` unresolved / bounded / debated

Do not replace this with a second hidden code. Drawing may attach provenance to a claim or trace a synthesis path, but glyph and text remain authoritative.

## Sources and References

Keep references academically conventional enough to scan and cite. Use subtle underlines, occasional braces, contribution notes, faint construction traces and light marginal annotations.

Avoid turning references into cards or making every citation colourful.

## Long-page rhythm

Design the entire page as a visual argument. A strong sequence may feel like:

**opening intensity → conceptual setup → visual explanation → interaction/conflict → interpretation → evidence → scope → limitations → provenance → sources**

The exact sequence may differ by record. What matters is dynamics.
