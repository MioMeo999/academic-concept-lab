# Skill — Academic Concept Lab Visual Language

## Purpose

Use this skill whenever creating, redesigning or refining visual interfaces for Academic Concept Lab.

## Core identity

> **A serious contemporary academic publication that has become a living research surface.**

> **TYPESET KNOWLEDGE + DRAWN THINKING**

Formal scholarship remains typeset. Drawing enters as visible intellectual work.

## Core rules

1. Clean white canvas.
2. Editorial typography carries scholarship.
3. Handwritten/drawn marks are selective intellectual interventions.
4. Colour behaves like pigment, not UI accent.
5. Materiality lives mainly in marks, not fake paper.
6. Theory may change composition.
7. Shared visual grammar, not shared page template.
8. Quiet scholarship remains recognisable.
9. No semantic mark without intellectual work.
10. Avoid a normal website with pencil drawings pasted on top.
11. Preserve the shared visual DNA while discovering geometry from each theory.
12. Keep live scholarship, theory-specific interaction and authored drawing as
    one explanatory system; do not flatten useful interaction into static art.

## Discovery-surface workflow

For a collection, index or discovery page, identify the reader's current
intention before choosing density and composition. Possible intentions include
**EXPLORE** (space), **FIND** (speed) and **ENTER A FIELD** (structure), but
these are options rather than a required three-state template. Do not assume
one fixed layout should survive every search, filter or navigation state.

When the collection is the content, information architecture itself may be the
primary visual expression. Do not add authored artwork merely to make the page
feel designed; let grouping, typography, white space, rules and interaction
state carry the visual work when that is the clearer choice.

For a personal collection or return surface, do not automatically inherit the
full discovery interface from its source collection. First ask what the reader
needs to recognise and resume. Prefer the smallest interface that preserves
recognition, direct return, removal, accessibility and truthful persistence.
Personal collections should earn complexity as their real scale and data model
require it; do not invent organisational features in anticipation of future
use.

## Theory-page workflow

Do not design yet. Before choosing artwork or a page template:

1. Ask what the reader most needs to distinguish, what they might misunderstand
   and what could be explored interactively.
2. Determine what must remain stable and what changes or is reinterpreted.
3. Only then determine states, controls, transitions, residual traces and
   relationship changes.
4. Decide what should be live HTML, authored art, interactive code or quiet
   scholarship.

Use `INTERACTION-GRAMMAR.md` for this model and its review questions.

Focus does not automatically mean disappearance. Preserve a non-focused state
as a residual trace when the theory says states coexist, and use movement only
when it makes a conceptual change visible. Semantic controls remain visible,
keyboard accessible, screen-reader legible, touch-usable and functional with
`prefers-reduced-motion`.

**DRAWN ONCE · READ MANY WAYS.** When a theory has one stable conceptual world,
consider whether one authored source field can support multiple readings before
commissioning separate illustrations. One source field may serve as **WORLD** (opening/orientation),
**CROP** (relationship inspection), **FRAGMENT** (local concept), **OVERLAY
SUBSTRATE** (live interaction) or **VIEWPOINT** (reinterpretation of the same
relation). This is optional; use separate authored art when the theory needs a
different world or one source would falsely unify unrelated concepts.

## Reference use

Before a major visual design or redesign, also read
`docs/visual-language/references/README.md` and inspect the relevant reference
group. Study the material vocabulary and page rhythm, then derive geometry from
the content. The reference library is documentation only: it is not a runtime
asset set, component catalogue or instruction to add artwork.

Use references to understand pigment, pressure, mark-making, layering, overlap, erasure, white space, scholarly typography and visual rhythm. Do not copy layouts or diagrams.

> **Creative assimilation, not imitation.**

## Approved benchmark principle

- **Home** is the site-level benchmark: an expansive knowledge atlas with the
  strongest opening gesture and the reference density rhythm.
- **Gestalt** is the Music Psychology benchmark: grouping, whole/part,
  relational perception and perceptual fields.
- **JD–R** is the Organisational Behaviour benchmark: workplace conditions,
  parallel currents, effort, support and changed relationships.
- **Person–Environment Fit** is the Organisational Behaviour benchmark for
  persistent authored-world / relational correspondence.
- **Library** is the knowledge-discovery and mode-aware information-density
  benchmark: one canonical collection, three reading intentions — Explore
  (space), Find (speed) and Enter a field (structure).
- **Saved** is the personal working-set / return-continuity benchmark: **A
  SMALL WORKING SET · RETURN WITH ALMOST NO FRICTION**. Keep it simpler than
  the Library and let recognition, direct return, reversible removal and
  persistence truth guide the interface.
- **About** is the epistemic-transparency benchmark: **SHOW HOW WE KNOW**.
  It uses reader-facing editorial geometry to explain knowledge kinds,
  provenance, source-to-record trails, evidence boundaries and uncertainty;
  do not treat it as a reusable page template.
- **Reflexive Thematic Analysis** is the frozen method benchmark:
  **REFLEXIVE / REVISABLE KNOWLEDGE** — **ANALYSIS LEAVES TRACES**. Its
  worktable, reflexivity/theme chapter, divergence field, methodological
  critique and quiet scholarly resolution are RTA-specific geometry, not a
  universal theory-page structure.

These roles describe transferable design problems, not reusable compositions.
Future theory pages inherit the global shell, typography, spacing, material
language, provenance language, art-intensity logic and quiet-scholarship
behaviour, then develop their own conceptual geometry.

The RTA benchmark also records that visual history can communicate revision,
authored artwork can establish human and conceptual presence as one material
layer, and a page's density rhythm can carry method meaning. Carry those
lessons forward only when the next method or theory warrants them; do not copy
the RTA worktable, divergence field or phase rail.

### AET benchmark lesson

The frozen AET route (`/aet-visual-rebuild`) is the benchmark for **temporal /
event-driven knowledge**: one person, one workday, many moments. **THE PAGE
REMEMBERS WHAT HAPPENED.** Its lesson is theory-specific: let event, reaction,
residue, later evaluation and time determine the geometry. The illustration
explains the lived experience while live HTML and the Relationship Ledger
preserve canonical structure. Its primary interaction is **READ THE MAP → READ
UNDER IT**. See
`docs/visual-language/DESIGN-SYSTEM.md` and `PAGE-BEHAVIOUR.md` for the full
freeze record; do not reuse AET's storyboard as a universal template.

## Final review

Check every page against `docs/visual-language/REVIEW-CHECKLIST.md` and the
interaction questions in `docs/visual-language/INTERACTION-GRAMMAR.md` before
presenting it.

## Hybrid art-direction rule

Do not assume expressive visual material must be recreated procedurally. For complex or painterly/pencil artwork, prefer generated/authored assets combined with editorial HTML/CSS and lightweight interactive SVG/Canvas where needed.

When a conceptual moment depends on expressive materiality, prefer an authored
or generated art asset over pseudo-pencil SVG/CSS. Keep formal claims and
scholarship live in HTML; use code for layout, state and genuine interaction.

Do not treat authored artwork as the finished interface by default. The art
may be a field, crop, fragment, state layer, figure or relational trace, while
semantic HTML carries the scholarship and lightweight code expresses the
theory-specific relationship.
