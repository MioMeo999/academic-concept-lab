# Academic Concept Lab — Visual Thesis

## Visual identity

Academic Concept Lab should not look like a conventional academic website with hand-drawn decoration added on top.

It should feel like:

> **A serious contemporary academic publication that has become a living research surface.**

The page has two simultaneous registers.

### Typeset knowledge
Carries titles, definitions, claims, explanations, evidence, citations, sources, limitations, scope, provenance and references. It should feel precise, editorial, contemporary and highly readable.

### Drawn thinking
Carries grouping, questioning, comparison, connection, reframing, revision, uncertainty, boundaries, competing interpretations and conceptual structure.

The two registers coexist without collapsing into one another. The page should feel designed by an editorial designer and actively worked through by a researcher.

## Desired feeling

- precise underneath
- human on top
- scholarly but not dry
- expressive but not decorative
- colourful but disciplined
- visually rich but controlled
- drawn but not childish
- contemporary rather than nostalgic
- exploratory without becoming chaotic

The reader should feel someone is actively **reading / drawing / testing / connecting / questioning / correcting / tracing / synthesising** inside the knowledge.

## White canvas

Use a clean contemporary white ground. Prefer white space and optical breathing room. Avoid aged notebook paper, beige stationery, scrapbook paper, vintage journal simulation and classroom worksheet aesthetics.

> **Grain lives in the mark, not the paper.**

## Theory should generate composition

Do not give every theory the same layout. Avoid universal formulas such as `hero + summary + cards + diagram + evidence + references`.

Different theories should produce different spatial behaviour. Gestalt may generate grouping, overlap and competing boundaries; Tonal Hierarchy may generate contextual role and changing organisation; Predictive Processing may generate recurrence, mismatch and revision; ITPRA may generate temporality, simultaneity and appraisal.

These are intellectual structures, not mandatory diagram shapes.

Ask:

> **How should this page behave because this theory behaves this way?**

## Visual rhythm

Long pages should not maintain one visual intensity. A strong page can move through:

**rich → quiet → dense → sparse → active → reflective → scholarly**

High-intensity zones may use more pigment, overlap, large conceptual figures or interaction. Quiet zones should use strong typography, white space, subtle traces, carefully placed provenance and minimal drawing.

Evidence, Scope, Limitations, Provenance, Sources and References should generally be calmer.

> **Quiet does not mean generic.**

## Digital-native behaviour

Do not imitate a printed notebook literally. Use browser affordances when they help thinking: comparison, reveal, contextual change, responsive recomposition, state transitions, provenance inspection and relationship exploration.

Interaction should help the reader think, not merely look impressive.

## Core anti-pattern

The biggest failure mode is:

> **a normal website with pencil drawings pasted on top**

If the underlying composition stays generic and the drawing does not participate in the intellectual structure, the visual language has not been realised.
