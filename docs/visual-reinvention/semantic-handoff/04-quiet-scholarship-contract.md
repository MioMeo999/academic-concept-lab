# Quiet scholarship contract

“Quiet scholarship” means the record remains rigorous and traceable without
turning every qualification into a competing headline. The contract below is
semantic, not visual.

## Evidence

**Function:** Show what a source or study actually did, found, and can support.

**Must remain visible:** Citation, source type, design when available, tested
question, finding/contribution, and what the source did not test.

**Main epistemic risk:** A compatible result is promoted into proof of a whole
theory, mechanism, neural implementation, or causal story.

**Distinctions:** Empirical study, process model, review, constructed teaching
example, and Concept Lab synthesis are not one evidence class.

**Reader should understand:** Evidence is bounded by method and measure.

**Non-loss rule:** Never remove `design`, `tested`, `found`, or `notTested` in a
condensed view without leaving an accessible equivalent.

## Scope

**Function:** State what a record explains well and where it stops.

**Must remain visible:** The `explains` and `stops` lists and the qualification
that the record is not a complete theory of its wider domain.

**Main epistemic risk:** A powerful lens becomes an encyclopedia, universal
law, or complete causal account.

**Distinctions:** Gestalt grouping is not all music cognition; Tonal Hierarchy
is not complete key finding; Predictive Processing is not all music or all
neural computation; ITPRA is not a complete emotion theory.

**Reader should understand:** Stopping points are part of the record’s identity.

**Non-loss rule:** Scope boundaries cannot be hidden behind optional expansion
or interaction completion.

## Limitations and qualifications

**Function:** Keep uncertainty, under-specification, construct boundaries, and
critical commentary attached to the claims they qualify.

**Must remain visible:** `qualifications`, `oversimplifications`, critical
review boundaries, and the local `?` notes.

**Main epistemic risk:** A clean explanatory arc erases contested mechanisms,
context dependence, material/task limits, or evidence gaps.

**Distinctions:** “Not established” is not the same as “false”; “constructed” is
not the same as “weak”; “influential” is not the same as “settled.”

**Reader should understand:** The record is careful because the knowledge is
complex, not because the record is unfinished.

**Non-loss rule:** A summary may shorten wording but may not remove the
qualification that changes the permissible inference.

## Provenance

**Function:** Show responsibility for every claim-like unit.

**Must remain visible:** The five glyph meanings—`●`, `■`, `▲`, `✦`, `?`—and
the note explaining what each mark covers.

**Main epistemic risk:** A teaching device or editorial synthesis is mistaken
for a source-authored diagram, primary finding, or objective measurement.

**Distinctions:** Source-grounded theory, empirical finding, constructed
example, editorial synthesis, and unresolved question.

**Reader should understand:** The platform is explicit about where its voice
enters the record.

**Non-loss rule:** Provenance must not be encoded by colour alone or omitted
from compact/mobile/linear versions.

## Sources

**Function:** Provide an actionable reading threshold and a fuller trail.

**Must remain visible:** Citation, contribution, DOI where recorded, minimum
reading label, and access to the full source list.

**Main epistemic risk:** A short reading list is mistaken for exhaustive
scholarship, or a citation is displayed without saying why it is present.

**Distinctions:** Minimum reading, full source trail, historical origin, review,
and evidence source.

**Reader should understand:** Each source has a defined responsibility in the
record.

**Non-loss rule:** Do not remove contribution notes or collapse all sources
into undifferentiated references.

## References and relations

**Function:** Situate the record in an intellectual neighbourhood without
making neighbouring records interchangeable.

**Must remain visible:** Related record title, relation type/direction, body
explanation, route, and relation provenance where available.

**Main epistemic risk:** “Related” becomes “same as,” or an editorial bridge is
mistaken for a historical or source-grounded relation.

**Distinctions:** Complements, contrasts, bridges, informs, formalises,
extends, and computationally implements.

**Reader should understand:** A relation tells them how two records can be read
together and what question remains distinct.

**Non-loss rule:** Never replace a typed relation with a generic “you may also
like” recommendation.

## Quiet scholarship acceptance test

A future presentation passes this contract only if a reader can answer, without
operating a control:

- What kind of record is this?
- What does it explain?
- What did the cited evidence actually test?
- What did it not test?
- Which statements are source claims, teaching constructions, or editorial
  synthesis?
- Where does the record stop?
- What remains unresolved?
- What should I read next, and why is it related?
