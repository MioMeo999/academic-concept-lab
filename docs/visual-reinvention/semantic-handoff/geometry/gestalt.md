# Style-neutral geometry: Gestalt Principles in Music

This is an abstract knowledge geometry, not a drawing brief.

## Primary structure

`events → candidate organisations → grouping / boundary / figure`

## Relationality

The unit of meaning is not an isolated event. Relations include temporal
proximity, similarity across several dimensions, continuation, and surrounding
configuration. The same event can have different perceptual roles in different
organisations.

## Hierarchy

`event → local group/motive → phrase → larger section`

The hierarchy is possible and defeasible. A local cue does not explain complete
musical form.

## Temporality and recurrence

Temporal spacing can create a candidate boundary; continuation preserves or
changes an organisation; groups can recur or become parts of larger groups.

## Feedback / mutual constraint

The whole constrains the role of a part while local cues contribute to the whole
that is experienced. This is a mutual part–whole relation, not a one-way causal
pipeline.

## Ambiguity and conflict

`cue X + cue Y → reinforcement | competition | unresolved ambiguity`

No single cue has a universal priority in the record.

## Context dependence

Material, surrounding pattern, listener experience, attention, and musical
structure affect the available organisation. Culture/experience is a boundary
condition, not an all-purpose explanation.

## Core transformation

Separate sensory events become related perceptual units; a part’s role changes
with the organisation that contains it.

## Core relational problem

How can multiple physical events be organised as a coherent or competing whole
without assuming one objectively correct boundary?

## Ratified historical relationships

Prägnanz has a three-part intellectual progression: a historical ambition
toward relatively good/simple organisation under prevailing conditions; a
critical problem because “good/simple” is under-specified without a stated
objective and conditions; and later reformulations including simplicity,
minimum principles, likelihood, Bayesian interpretations, and
self-organisation. Later frameworks must remain identifiable as later
reformulations or extensions rather than unqualified classical Gestalt
content.

The Gestalt → GTTM lineage has four distinct stages: broad Gestalt perceptual
organisation; music-specific adaptation of grouping ideas; GTTM preference rules
as a later formal, defeasible music-cognitive system; and later empirical work
comparing rule-derived predictions with listener segmentation. Do not collapse
the stages, identify GTTM with classical Gestalt, or treat later GTTM listener
studies as direct validation of classical Gestalt psychology.

This is a semantic relationship and boundary statement only. It does not
prescribe a timeline, staircase, arrows, stages, cards, or any other visual
implementation.

## Invariants for future presentation

Keep cue competition, gradedness, part–whole dependence, hierarchy, and the
distinction between grouping and expectancy. Do not reduce this geometry to a
law list or an unqualified single path.
