## Core problem

What happens before an expected musical event, at its arrival, and after it has
been interpreted? How can anticipation, preparation, prediction, immediate
reaction, and slower appraisal coexist without becoming one undifferentiated
feeling or a rigid processing sequence?

## Central claim

Huron proposes five functionally distinct response systems: Imagination,
Tension, Prediction, Reaction, and Appraisal. They have different temporal
positions and functions around an outcome. Prediction and Reaction can begin in
parallel after onset; Appraisal is slower and context-sensitive. The acronym is
not a mandatory `I → T → P → R → A` algorithm.

## Primary concepts

- Imagination: possible futures, motivation, and deferred gratification
- Tension: preparation as uncertainty, importance, and imminence change
- Prediction: after-onset evaluation of predictive accuracy
- Reaction: rapid conservative response to possible danger/opportunity
- Appraisal: broader, slower, revisable contextual evaluation
- outcome onset as temporal hinge
- contrastive valence as a possible, not universal, route to positive experience
- schematic, veridical, dynamic, and conscious expectation sources
- source concurrence and source conflict
- expectation, surprise, affect, learning, and context

## Mechanism and relationships

The repository supports this abstract architecture:

`expectation sources → pre-outcome imagination/tension → outcome onset →
prediction ∥ reaction → slower/recurring appraisal`

The response systems may differ in valence or timing. A later appraisal can
confirm, revise, or reverse an earlier response. The source layer is parallel to
the response timeline; schematic, veridical, dynamic, and conscious are not
four stages before Imagination.

## Important distinctions

- Prediction is formed before onset; Prediction Response evaluates accuracy
  after onset.
- Prediction and Reaction are parallel post-outcome questions, not sequential
  steps mandated by the acronym.
- Tension Response is not harmonic dissonance, generic subjective musical
  tension, or GTTM prolongational tension.
- Appraisal is not simply a liking rating.
- Expected does not mean pleasant and unexpected does not mean unpleasant
  overall.
- Contrastive valence is Huron’s proposal, not a universal pleasure mechanism.
- Expectancy-violation evidence does not validate five isolated physiological
  modules.
- ITPRA is not predictive processing, IDyOM, a complete learning theory, or a
  complete emotion theory.

## Temporal and hierarchical structure

Imagination may extend far before the outcome; Tension operates near the
approaching hinge; Prediction and Reaction begin after onset; Appraisal forms a
slower tail that can recur. The model is temporal and functional, not a fixed
neural hierarchy.

## Evidence structure

| Evidence | What it proposes, tests, or supports | Boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Huron (2006) | Five functionally distinct response systems, proposed functions, expectation sources, prediction effect, and contrastive valence. | Not proof of five isolated physiological modules or a complete computable emotion model. |
| Steinbeis, Koelsch & Sloboda (2006) | Subjective, physiological, and neural consequences of expected/unexpected harmonic events. | Not a direct test of five ITPRA systems or contrastive valence as a full mechanism. |
| Koelsch et al. (2008) | ERP and skin-conductance differences for unexpected chords in naturalistic contexts. | Not a direct demonstration of the five systems. |
| Pearce (2018) | Learned musical regularities and probabilistic prediction as a bridge from exposure to expectation. | Not the complete ITPRA architecture or a one-to-one mapping to response systems. |
| Cheung et al. (2019) | Nonlinear relationship among uncertainty, surprise, pleasure, and brain activity. | Not a direct test of Huron’s sequence or separate Prediction/Reaction/Appraisal systems. |
| Aversa (2009) | Critical boundary against treating expectation as the only route to musical pleasure. | Not a primary empirical test of ITPRA. |
| Constructed timing/lens examples | Distinguish timing, outcome onset, and functional questions while holding the safe event fixed. | No physiological, emotional, or individual-state measurement. |

## Scope and stopping points

Useful for pre-outcome anticipation/preparation, predictive success/failure,
parallel immediate response questions, slower contextual appraisal,
mixed-valence responses, expectation sources, and links among structure,
learning, expectation, and affect.

Stops short of a complete emotion theory, neural model, exact prediction content
for every listener, complete learning theory, universal explanation of musical
pleasure, validation of five isolated modules, or a substitute for predictive
processing/IDyOM.

## Interaction intent

The timing example changes outcome onset while holding the musical outcome and
context constant. The fixed-event lens switch changes only the interpretive
question. The purpose is to make temporal and functional distinctions visible;
the reader’s selection cannot be interpreted as tension, reaction, appraisal,
reinforcement, or physiology.
