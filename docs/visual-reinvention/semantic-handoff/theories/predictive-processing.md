# Theory manifest: Predictive Processing in Music

## Identity

- Record kind: `theory`
- Knowledge form: `framework`
- Qualifier: `Integrative predictive theory family`
- Discipline: `music-psych`
- Branch: `expectation-prediction`

- Core record identity: a broad predictive framework applied to music

  explanation of music.

## Core problem

How can music perception be understood as an ongoing interaction among
generative hypotheses, sensory input, mismatch, uncertainty, precision,
learning, attention, and possible action? Which parts of that account are
specified by evidence, and which remain a family of theoretical proposals?

## Central claim

Perception can be modelled as an interaction between predictions about hidden or
lower-level states and incoming sensory evidence. Prediction error can carry
information for inference, attention, learning, or model revision; precision
changes how much that mismatch influences the current inference. In music,
temporal and hierarchical examples are useful, but no single fixed cortical or
musical hierarchy is established by the record.

## Primary concepts

- predictive processing, predictive coding, and Predictive Coding of Music
- generative model and hidden/latent causes
- top-down prediction and lower-level/sensory state
- prediction error as model–input mismatch, not failure or dislike
- precision as reliability/influence weighting; under Gaussian assumptions,
  related to inverse variance
- first-order content prediction versus second-order precision prediction
- attention as possible precision modulation, not identical to precision
- omission as predicted input versus actual silence
- levels/timescales: acoustic feature, onset, pitch, rhythm, meter, melody,
  harmony, phrase, and style as explanatory levels
- active inference/action and sensory sampling as subordinate extensions
- PCM history, expertise, learning, culture, and critical mechanistic limits
- boundary with IDyOM information content and entropy

## Mechanism and relationships

The record supports this teaching abstraction:

`learned experience + current context → generative model → prediction ↔ actual
input → prediction error × estimated precision → update / attention / action →
new predictions`

This is a Concept Lab synthesis, not a literal cortical circuit. The message
direction is meaningful; the mapping of musical levels to anatomy is not fixed.

## Important distinctions

- Predictive processing is broader than next-note guessing.
- A generative model is not generative AI.
- Predictive-coding error is not IDyOM information content.
- IDyOM entropy is not predictive precision.
- Precision is not conscious confidence and attention is not proven to be only
  precision weighting.
- MMN/MMNm, ERAN, P3-related signals, and omission responses are not prediction
  error by definition.
- PCM is a music-specific formulation within the wider family, not the whole
  family and not one unchanged model.
- Zero error is not maximum pleasure.

## Temporal, hierarchical, and feedback structure

Prediction is ongoing and can concern future events or expected sensory
activity under a current hypothesis. Musical prediction can operate across
fast/local and slower/higher explanatory levels. The feedback relation is
recursive: an update alters what can be predicted next. Context, experience,
culture, and attention modulate the interpretation without being reduced to a
single late stage.

## Evidence structure

| Evidence | What it tests or supports | Boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Vuust et al. (2009) | Rhythmic incongruity and expertise differences in MMNm/P3am responses. | Not a complete predictive-processing circuit, neural precision parameter, or causal training result. |
| Ishida, Ishida & Nittono (2024) | Omission-period information about expected-note identity, with familiar-context differences and the reported decoding distinction. | Not proof of the full architecture or a uniquely identified pure prediction-error signal; familiarity conditions differed in more than melody. |
| Rohrmeier & Koelsch (2012) | Critical boundary: feature and temporal prediction are not equally specified for polyphony, formal structure, and higher-order prediction. | Not a rejection of all prediction accounts. |
| Koelsch, Vuust & Friston (2019) | Music-focused account of content prediction, precision prediction, attention, and active listening. | Theoretical account, not complete mechanistic validation. |
| Vuust et al. (2022) and cross-cultural exchange | Expanded PCM synthesis and the importance of culture/history of priors. | Cross-cultural validity remains unsettled. |
| Furutachi & Hofer (2026) | Current critical pressure for stronger mechanistic discrimination and careful interpretation of prediction-error responses. | Does not debunk predictive processing. |
| Constructed precision/omission interactions | Same deviation under different uncertainty and predicted event versus silence. | No neural response measurement, diagnosis, or published-stimulus replication. |

## Scope and stopping points

Useful for context-sensitive perception, musical expectation, sensory prediction,
violation responses, uncertainty, learning, attention, rhythm and meter, some
groove, expertise, omission, and perception–action coupling.

Stops short of a complete account of all music, emotion, reward, aesthetics,
culture, social interaction, creativity, or every neural computation.

## Interaction intent

The precision comparison holds the target event, pitch, duration, timbre, gain,
and displacement constant while changing the constructed uncertainty envelope.
The omission comparison holds the preceding beat pattern and expected onset
constant while changing whether the event arrives. The epistemic purpose is to
make model-relative mismatch and weighting discussable, not to infer the
reader’s neural activity.
