## Part 1 — Reference interpretation

Each reference is identified below by its visible title and contents.

### 1.1 "Academic Concept Lab — Material, Pigment & Mark Library"

*The atlas of pencil pressure, hatching directions, cross-hatching, scumble/stipple/smudge, pigment clouds, colour swatches, directional force fields, rubbed-out marks, ghost constructions, double-pass circles, partial boundaries, wavering underlines, box notes, highlight swipes, curve families, connector styles, layered overlaps, radial fields, gravitational centres, tonal-distance rings, ambiguity marks, competing-force marks, and texture swatches.* Highest material authority.

**Material lessons.**

1. *A mark records the act that made it.* Direction, speed, number of passes, interrupted contact, and applied pressure all remain readable after the fact. Nothing else in a digital interface has this property. A rectangle does not remember being drawn; a hatch does.
2. *Material is graded, not binary.* Whisper → light → medium → firm → heavy is a continuum with no natural step. So is broken → partial → closed boundary. So is single pass → double pass → loose multiple.
3. *Tone is accumulated, not declared.* Hatching does not set a darkness value; it reaches one by repetition. Scumble reaches it by broken repetition. Stipple by discrete events. These are three different *arguments* for the same darkness, and they look different because they were arrived at differently.
4. *Overlap can be non-destructive.* Transparent layering keeps its constituents identifiable. Two hatch directions in one area remain two directions, not one grey.
5. *Removal leaves residue.* Rubbed-out, partial erase, lifted pigment, and scratch-back are all different relationships to a former state, and none of them is deletion.
6. *Construction can be kept.* Guides, basic forms, iteration lines, perspective lines, and proportion grids are the visible scaffolding of a made thing.
7. *Incompleteness is expressible.* Open circle, broken box, corner marks, and open shape are all ways of saying "this is a boundary I am not fully asserting."

**Compositional lessons.** The atlas's real compositional teaching is *disciplined comparison*: a family of variants presented under identical conditions so that a small difference becomes visible. That is a serious idea and it recurs later in this document. Its grid of ruled compartments is not the lesson; it is a specimen-presentation convention appropriate to a specimen sheet.

**Intellectual lessons.** Material can carry the *status* of a statement rather than its content. An interrupted boundary can say "provisional." Retained construction can say "this was built for teaching." Two coexisting hatch directions can say "two readings are open." A residue can say "this was revised, and here is what it was." These are conventions to be **declared and tested**, not meanings that graphite intrinsically possesses.

**Do not copy literally.** The numbered families are not a component library. The pressure ladder is not a design token scale. Sections 07 (Directional Force Fields), 18 (Radial Fields), 19 (Gravitational Centres), 20 (Tonal Distance Rings) and 22 (Competing-Force Marks) are the most seductive and the most dangerous: they are attractive pictures of physics, and this project has almost no licence to assert physics. The atlas's own captions — *weight*, *intensity*, *influence*, *attractor* — are naive folk-semantics written for a swatch sheet and must not be imported as meanings.

**Risks of misunderstanding.**

- **Density reads as truth.** A reader will interpret a darker, denser, more worked mark as *more established*, *more probable*, *a larger effect*, or *more brain activity*. This is the single largest epistemic hazard in the entire visual direction, and the freeze forbids exactly this ("no visual form may imply a stronger mechanism, historical link, causal relation, effect, or boundary than the evidence supports").
- **Faintness reads as refutation.** A ghost or a residue will be read as *discredited*. The contract explicitly distinguishes "not established" from "false" and "constructed" from "weak."
- **An incomplete boundary reads as missing content** rather than as a claim about defeasibility.
- Taken as a whole, the atlas invites a *swatch aesthetic*: many small marks in compartments, i.e. decoration at high density. That is the opposite of what the Diagram Semantics Audit just demanded.

### 1.2 "Academic Concept Lab — Ideas, drawn together"

*The reference containing "A living atlas of theories, connections, and questions", "Colour System (Pigments for Thought)", "Hand-drawn Elements (A Visual Vocabulary)", sample pages for Gestalt Principles, Predictive Processing and Tonal Hierarchy, and quieter Evidence / Related Theories / References panels.* Strong authority for the spirit of the visual world.

**Material lessons.** Chromatic drawing can be *rich and controlled at once*. Colour here is saturated but restricted; marks are energetic but each has a beginning and an end. Nothing is airbrushed. The white ground is pure and load-bearing — it is not a background, it is the space relations happen in.

**Compositional lessons.**

1. *Precise typesetting and imperfect marks can share a page without either apologising.* The display serif is unhedged; the pencil is unhedged; they are simply in different registers.
2. *Different theories behave differently.* Three sample records are visibly not the same template. This is the single most important lesson in the reference set.
3. *Register changes exist.* The Evidence / Related Theories / References areas are quieter — more numbered, more ruled, less chromatic — without becoming a different website.

**Intellectual lessons.** Visible intellectual intervention is legitimate. A page can show that someone has *worked on* this material rather than merely transcribed it. That is the correct reading of "hand-drawn" for this project: not warmth, not craft nostalgia, but **evidence of intellectual labour**.

**Do not copy literally.** The three sample theory pages must not be read as wireframes. The Gestalt sample is not a Gestalt layout; the Tonal sample is not a Tonal layout; the Predictive Processing sample is not a Predictive Processing layout. Their theory captions are reference-image text and are not academic sources. Their shared shell — left contents rail, hero title with a chromatic illustration beside it, then sections — is a conventional documentation layout with drawings inserted into it, which is precisely the outcome the brief rejects.

**Risks of misunderstanding.** The most likely failure here is *one hero illustration per theory*. That reproduces the pattern the audit dismantled: 24 of 26 structures said nothing the prose did not already say. Differentiation must be **structural** — how the page is organised, what it lets you change, where it goes quiet — not **illustrative**. A second risk is treating the sample sidebars ("Overview / Key Principles / In Music / Evidence / Limitations / Connections / References") as a settled information architecture; the handoff explicitly leaves that open ([open questions](./semantic-handoff/06-open-questions.md), cross-project questions 1 and 2).

### 1.3 "Academic Concept Lab — Think · Connect · See Theories"

*The broad moodboard containing Colour Palette, Material & Texture, Visual Spirit, a Predictive Processing sample, Concept Map, Annotation Language, Visual Building Blocks, Quiet Scholarship, and a Moodboard strip.* Supporting reference.

**Material lessons.** Chromatic abundance is possible without becoming childish if each colour is doing a job. Its "Material & Texture" area is honest about the difference between a *pigment* and a *surface*, which is useful — and its surfaces are the part to leave behind.

**Compositional lessons.** Its most valuable panel is **Annotation Language**: the proposal that marks have *declared meanings*, listed openly, so the reader is not decoding a private code. A declared notation is exactly right for a project whose whole apparatus is about knowing who is speaking.

**Intellectual lessons.** Conceptual visibility is a goal in itself — the reference is arguing that thought should be *inspectable*, not merely reported. That matches the handoff's insistence that uncertainty is content rather than a defect.

**Do not copy literally.** Its annotation assignments are the wrong vocabulary. *Circle = key idea. Underline = emphasis. Star = important. Bracket = grouping.* That is a **rhetoric of emphasis** — it tells the reader what to care about. Academic Concept Lab needs a **notation of epistemic status** — what a statement *is* and what may be inferred from it. "Important" is not a claim class; `●`, `■`, `▲`, `✦`, `?` are. Also resist, as the brief instructs: sticky notes, tape, torn paper, notebook props, the card grid, the poster/worksheet register, and the moodboard strip itself.

**Risks of misunderstanding.** Read carelessly, this reference produces a digital scrapbook: an abundance of expressive artefacts with no differentiated responsibility. Its "Quiet Scholarship" panel is also a warning — there, quiet scholarship is four small icons in a box, which is quiet as *decoration*, not quiet as *rigour*.

### 1.4 The combined language inferred

Reading the three together, and discounting each one's characteristic failure mode, the shared instruction is:

> **Typeset text carries the scholarship. Marks carry the status of the scholarship. White space is the medium in which relations are held. Material intensity records the work that has been done, not the truth of what is said. Every convention is declared.**

Four working principles follow, which govern all three hypotheses in Part 3.

**P1 — No mark without a difference.** A mark is drawn only where it encodes a distinction the prose asserts and the evidence licenses. If removing it removes no information, it should not exist. This is the direct design consequence of the audit's 24/26 finding, and it is the strongest defence against re-creating the problem the audit found.

**P2 — Intensity encodes work, not truth.** Accumulation, density, and pressure may express *how much investigation exists* — number of converging sources, how much of a chain has been specified, how much of a comparison has been made. They may never express probability, effect size, confidence, or correctness. A famously well-studied claim and a fragile one can be equally dark; what differs is what the marks *are*.

**P3 — Every convention is declared and has a text equivalent.** No material operation may be the only carrier of a distinction, per the [quiet scholarship contract](./semantic-handoff/04-quiet-scholarship-contract.md). Each convention needs a local statement of what it means *and* of what it does not license. Colour never carries provenance alone.

**P4 — Grain lives in the mark, not the paper.** Retained from the brief and endorsed. Surfaces stay clean; the whole material argument happens in what was drawn.

## Part 2 — Project reading

Described only from the frozen handoff.

### 2.1 What kind of knowledge world this is

Three things stand out from the handoff, and they are not the things a normal "academic website" brief would produce.

**First: this is a jurisdiction of claims, not a body of information.** The [scholarly apparatus](./semantic-handoff/02-scholarly-apparatus.md) does not organise content by topic; it organises it by *who is responsible for it and what may be inferred from it*. Nine claim classes. Five provenance marks. `tested` / `found` / `notTested` kept separately. "Not established" is not "false." "Constructed" is not "weak." "Influential" is not "settled." The atomic unit of this project is not a fact; it is **a statement with an owner and a permitted inference range**.

**Second: every reader action is an experimental design.** The [interaction intents](./semantic-handoff/03-interaction-intents.md) document specifies each interaction as *action / variable / invariant / what the reader should notice / what may not be inferred*. That is not a UX table. That is a methods section. No other knowledge product I can think of publishes its own controls for its teaching examples.

**Third — and this is the finding that shapes everything below — all four flagship theories have the same deep structure.** Read the four geometries side by side:

| Record | What is held | What changes | What the change reveals |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Gestalt | the same events | temporal spacing, register, or both | the same events support different organisations |
| Tonal Hierarchy | the same physical C4 | the preceding tonal context | the same pitch takes a different tonal role |
| Predictive Processing | the same deviation | the assumed precision | the same mismatch has different model-relative significance |
| ITPRA | the same outcome | when, and with which question, you ask | one event yields different functional answers |

Four different literatures, four different centuries of argument, one shared structure: **role is a function of conditions, and the thing itself does not change.** The handoff even states the invariant abstractly — "context is part of meaning… a pitch, event, cue, or prediction cannot be detached from the context the record says gives it a role."

And the project's *scholarship* has the same structure. An evidence X-ray is a statement of what a study held constant, what it varied, and therefore what it licenses. The reason the four theories and the apparatus feel like one project is that they are the same operation performed at two levels: **conditions on a percept, conditions on a claim.**

That convergence is the identity of Academic Concept Lab. It is not a style; it is what this knowledge *is*.

### 2.2 Why "forces on the page" is the wrong primitive

The brief proposes that ideas should exert forces on the page. I think this should be rejected, for three reasons.

1. **Force asserts a mechanism the evidence does not license.** If a mass attracts, something is being claimed about strength, direction, and influence. The freeze forbids visual forms that imply a stronger mechanism, causal relation, or effect than the evidence supports. Tonal Hierarchy explicitly is not a key-finding process; a gravitational tonic *asserts* one. Predictive Processing explicitly has no fixed cortical hierarchy; a flowing force field *asserts* one. The metaphor is charming and it quietly converts four carefully bounded records into physics.
2. **Force makes the idea the agent and the page the recipient.** That is a theatrical relationship. It produces movement, drama, and eventually decoration — and it gives the reader nothing to do but watch.
3. **Force has no quiet register.** Evidence, Scope, Limitations, Provenance and Sources do not exert forces. Under this thesis they can only be the parts where the visual idea stops, which guarantees that quiet scholarship becomes the leftover.

**The replacement primitive is *condition*.**

> Academic Concept Lab is a place where something is held still and its conditions are changed, and where every change of condition is declared.

Condition is better on all three counts. It asserts nothing the records do not already assert — it is *literally* the shared structure of all four geometries and of the evidence chain. It makes the reader the agent rather than the spectator. And it has a native quiet register, because a source, a study, and a scope statement are all condition statements: *this held, under these controls, within these limits.*

Material has an obvious and honest job under this primitive. Drawing's unique competence is that **the same mark against a changed field is visibly a different thing while being demonstrably the same mark.** No other medium makes constancy and difference perceptible simultaneously. That is not decoration; it is the argument.

### 2.3 What makes this different from its neighbours

| Neighbour | Its operation | What Academic Concept Lab does instead |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Wikipedia** | Flattens every claim into one neutral voice, with responsibility exported to footnotes. | Refuses a single voice. Source claim, reported finding, paraphrase, platform explanation, teaching construction, editorial synthesis, critique, proposed connection, and open question stay distinguishable at the level of the sentence. |
| **A textbook** | Resolves. Its job is to leave the reader with the settled version. | Keeps the unresolved unresolved *as content*, and states where each record stops. Every manifest has a "stops at" list that is part of the record's identity, not an apology. |
| **A note-taking app** | Private, unaccountable, about the note-taker; marks mean whatever the owner meant. | Editorially responsible and attributable. A mark here has an owner and a declared meaning, and the reader's own ratings are explicitly labelled as learner-generated and non-diagnostic. |
| **A normal academic website** | Presents outputs — abstracts, papers, findings. | Presents the *conditions under which a claim holds*, and what the same study did not test. |
| **A diagram library** | Asserts structure through shapes; a diagram's authority comes from looking authoritative. | Has just audited its own diagrams and found 24 of 26 semantically redundant. It must now earn every mark. This is possibly the only knowledge project that has formally revoked its own illustrations. |
| **A course platform** | Progress, completion, assessment; the reader is measured. | Forbids reader action being read as measurement of perception, ability, physiology, or emotion. There is no score. **Nothing you do here is about you.** |

### 2.4 What a reader should feel they are doing

Not *studying a topic*. **Handling a claim.**

Picking something up, turning it, changing what surrounds it, watching what its role becomes, and being told each time exactly what was held still. Then — in a different and calmer register — being able to ask *who says so, on what evidence, tested how, and where does this stop.*

The feeling to aim for is **intellectual traction**: the sense that the material has resistance, that care is being taken because the knowledge is genuinely difficult, and that the site is not trying to leave you with a tidy conclusion. The feeling to avoid is *fluency* — the frictionless, well-designed clarity that makes a reader believe a contested field is settled.

## Part 3 — Three visual hypotheses

Three genuinely different answers to *what the interface is*: an **apparatus**, a **surface**, and an **edition**.

*Also considered and set aside:* a **musical score** register (a time axis with parallel staves) — excellent for ITPRA and Predictive Processing, structurally wrong for Gestalt and for all quiet scholarship, and a category error for a music-*psychology* project, which would end up looking like a music site; an **infinite canvas / territory** model — genuinely digital-native, but it fights long-form reading, the contents-parity requirement, and the linear fallback obligation, and it has become a generic AI-web pattern; and a **force field**, rejected in Part 2.

---

### Hypothesis A — The Conditioned Specimen

**One sentence.** *Academic Concept Lab behaves visually like a laboratory plate whose caption is the argument: something is held still in the open, its conditions are changed around it, and both the constancy and the change are drawn.*

**World model.** A bright, quiet, mostly white plate. Very little furniture: no cards, no panels, no boxes with backgrounds. Content sits directly on the ground because the ground is the field in which a condition applies. The page's characteristic sight is **the same drawn thing appearing more than once, identically, in differently conditioned surrounds** — and a small, exact typeset statement of what was held and what changed. It should feel closer to a comparative plate in a scientific atlas than to a document, but with the caption promoted to the status of the argument.

**Spatial logic.** Two axes with distinct meanings.
- *Across* the page: **conditions of one question.** Two, three, or four registered fields. Registration is the point — the invariant must occupy the identical position in each, so that difference is perceptible rather than described.
- *Down* the page: **successive questions.** Each new question re-declares its own invariant and variable. A record's structure is therefore a *sequence of controlled questions*, and the record's shape is determined by how many genuinely distinct questions its geometry contains — which is different for every theory.

Intellectual structure is allowed to affect composition in one specific way: **the number, kind, and relationship of a theory's conditions determine the page's geometry.** Not its illustrations.

**Typography / drawing relationship.** Typeset text carries every claim, every boundary, and every control statement. Drawing does exactly one job: **it differences.** It shows what changed between conditions and what did not. A mark that is not participating in a comparison does not get drawn. This is P1 at full strength, and it is why this hypothesis is the most resistant to relapse into decoration.

The invariant should ideally be typeset or drawn *identically* — the same path, the same glyph — never redrawn "by hand" per condition, because a hand-varied invariant destroys the claim of constancy. That is a useful discipline: **the constant must be mechanically constant.**

**Material behaviour.**
- *The held mark* — one identical mark, repeated without variation across conditions.
- *The field* — hatching, direction, and density belong to the surround, and only the surround changes. This is honest simultaneous contrast: the same graphite genuinely looks different against different hatching, which is the visual fact the whole hypothesis rests on.
- *Accumulation* records how many conditions have been examined (P2), never how strong a result is.
- *Partial boundaries* mark the edge of a condition's applicability — the scope statement drawn as an edge that is explicitly not closed.
- *Erasure* is reserved and rare: it marks a condition that was withdrawn.
- Colour distinguishes **voice**, not strength: which claim class is speaking. Never colour alone.

**Theory differentiation.** This falls out of the geometries rather than being invented.

- **Gestalt** — conditions are *cue configurations*, and the crucial fact is that they can be **non-exclusive**: reinforcement, competition, and unresolved ambiguity are three different relationships between conditions. Gestalt's page therefore needs conditions that can be shown *simultaneously overlaid*, not only side by side, because ambiguity is the case where one condition supports two organisations at once. The Prägnanz progression and the Gestalt → GTTM lineage are not conditions at all; they are *stages of an intellectual history*, and they must be handled by a different device (see stress test, Part 4).
- **Tonal Hierarchy** — the purest case, and the one that needs the strongest discipline about levels. Three distinct things must never be composited: an observed judgment, an aggregated profile, and a psychological interpretation. The page can only be honest if these appear as **three separate registers**, differently drawn. The record forbids exact profile values, so no measured chart may be drawn at all: only a qualitative grouping. The key neighbourhood is a further register — a *representation*, explicitly non-literal — and should not share visual grammar with the probe comparison at all, or it will inherit its apparent measuredness.
- **Predictive Processing** — the conditions are *assumptions*, not stimuli. What changes is the assumed uncertainty; what is held is the physical deviation. This makes the page distinctive: its variable is invisible. The interesting design problem is drawing an assumption honestly — a declared envelope with declared constructed values, marked `▲`, that cannot be mistaken for a measurement. Omission is the sharpest case: an expectation frame with **no mark inside it**, which only reads as an event because the frame was declared first.
- **ITPRA** — the hardest fit, and therefore the best test. Its variable is *when the question is asked*, and its structure has a genuine simultaneity (Prediction ∥ Reaction) that a comparison of conditions cannot express. See Part 4; this is where Hypothesis A needs help.

**Quiet scholarship.** The strongest feature. An evidence X-ray *is already* a conditions statement: design (what was held), tested (what varied), found (what was observed), notTested (what the conditions do not license). It should be set as the **calm, unhatched, fully typeset sibling** of the loud comparison — same intellectual grammar, opposite material temperature. Sources are condition statements too: each carries a stated contribution — *this source is responsible for this*. Scope is drawn as a boundary that is deliberately not closed. Provenance is not a badge system but the statement of *who conditioned this*.

This means quiet scholarship is not a leftover: it is the same idea in a lower material register, which is exactly the rhythm the brief asks for.

**Annotation philosophy.** An annotation is earned when it states something about a claim that the claim cannot state about itself: what was held, what may not be inferred, who is speaking, or that a matter is unresolved. Annotations never restate, summarise, emphasise, or encourage. "Important" is not an annotation. `?` is.

**Interaction philosophy.** An interaction is justified when it lets the reader **change a condition and observe the consequence for a held constant** — nothing else. Reveals, expansions, and transitions do not qualify unless they change a condition. Critically, the *default* state must already be a stated comparison, not an empty control: the [quiet scholarship acceptance test](./semantic-handoff/04-quiet-scholarship-contract.md) requires eight questions to be answerable without operating anything.

**Long-page rhythm.** Loud where conditions are being compared; quiet where the record states what it cannot do. Density rises with the number of conditions in play and falls to nothing between questions. White space between questions is structural — it is the reset that lets the next invariant read as a new constant rather than a continuation.

**Digital-native quality.** Print can show two conditions; it cannot let you *change* one while the invariant demonstrably does not move. That single capability — visible constancy under reader-controlled change — is the whole digital argument, and it is epistemic rather than decorative.

**Scalability to 100+ theories.** Good, with a caveat. "Conditions" is a *content type* — a new record needs a declared comparison in data, not a bespoke illustration. That scales far better than per-theory artwork. The caveat is that not all knowledge is comparative; a purely historical or purely definitional record would have nothing to hold and nothing to vary, and would need a second mode.

**Risks.**
- **Monotony.** Every theory becomes an A/B toggle. This is the primary risk and it is serious.
- **Product-comparison resemblance.** Registered columns with a control above them is also what a pricing table looks like.
- **Museum vitrine.** Excessive reverence; specimens behind glass; cold.
- **Over-application.** Manufacturing a comparison where the knowledge has none, purely to fit the system. This would be a direct violation of the protocol's "choose the structure from the knowledge, not from a template."
- **Registration fetish.** The visual pleasure of exact repetition may start driving content decisions.

### Hypothesis B — The Working Surface

**One sentence.** *Academic Concept Lab behaves visually like a surface that keeps every state its argument has been in, so that revision, supersession, and coexistence are inspectable rather than reported.*

**World model.** One continuous ground on which nothing is ever deleted, only superseded. The current reading sits at full pressure; earlier formulations, competing readings, withdrawn assumptions, and the scaffolding of a teaching construction remain present at lower pressure. The characteristic sight is **depth**: several states occupying the same footprint, distinguishable by pressure and by the kind of mark rather than by position.

**Spatial logic.** Structure is in **z**, not in columns. Where Hypothesis A puts alternatives side by side, this puts them on top of each other, in register. Intellectual structure affects composition through *stratification*: what supersedes what, what remains active underneath, and what was built as scaffolding rather than claimed.

**Typography / drawing relationship.** Both stratify. A superseded formulation may remain as ghosted typesetting with visible construction lines; the current formulation is set at full black. Drawing's job is to show the *relationship between strata* — that this was revised into that, that this remains open beneath that.

**Material behaviour.** The richest of the three, and the most dangerous.
- *Pressure* = standing in the current reading. Explicitly **not** truth, and this must be stated relentlessly, because a reader's default reading of "faint" is "wrong."
- *Retained trace* = a prior state, kept inspectable.
- *Ghost construction* = scaffolding — a teaching construction (`▲`), or the visible skeleton of an editorial arrangement (`✦`).
- *Erasure with residue* = an attributable revision by the platform.
- *Two hatch directions in one area* = two readings genuinely held open, neither reduced to the other. This is the device Gestalt ambiguity needs.
- *Accumulation* = amount of scholarship deposited over time.

**Theory differentiation.**

- **Gestalt** — the natural home. The Prägnanz progression (ambition → under-specification → later reformulations) and the four-stage Gestalt → GTTM lineage are *literally* about strata that must not be collapsed, and the freeze's language is stratigraphic: later frameworks must remain identifiable *as later*. Ambiguity is also native, because two organisations of the same events can be shown coexisting rather than alternating.
- **Tonal Hierarchy** — dynamic updating is native: earlier context remains relevant while new context changes the balance, which is exactly a retained trace at reducing pressure. But the profile/process boundary is *harder* here than in A, because a stratified surface implies a temporal process, and the record insists a profile is not a process.
- **Predictive Processing** — recurrence is native: the previous model remains visible beneath the updated one, which is a real strength. Precision is not native at all — an assumption weight is not a stratum, and forcing it into layering would misrepresent it.
- **ITPRA** — appraisal is native and beautifully so. The handoff says a later appraisal can confirm, revise, or reverse an earlier response *without erasing the prior prediction or reaction*. That is a retained trace, stated in the source contract. But Prediction ∥ Reaction parallelism is again badly served, because stacking implies sequence.

**Quiet scholarship.** Provenance is the strongest fit of any hypothesis: "who put this mark here and when" is the organising question of a palimpsest. Sources become sediment; limitations become the parts deliberately left unconsolidated. But evidence suffers: an X-ray is a flat, precise, four-field statement, and stratification adds nothing to it — it can only obscure it.

**Annotation philosophy.** An annotation is earned when it marks a *change of state*: this was revised, this is superseded, this remains open beneath. Annotation is essentially historiographic.

**Interaction philosophy.** Justified when it lets the reader move between strata — bring a superseded formulation forward, inspect what a current claim replaced, or separate two coexisting readings. This is genuinely epistemic and print cannot do it.

**Long-page rhythm.** Risky. Because depth is everywhere, the surface tends toward uniform richness, and uniform richness is the enemy of rhythm. Quiet has to be manufactured as an exception rather than emerging from the system.

**Digital-native quality.** Excellent in principle: a printed palimpsest is fixed, whereas here the reader controls which stratum is foremost. This is the most interesting interaction idea of the three.

**Scalability.** Poor. Each stratification is bespoke — it depends on the particular intellectual history of a particular record. At 100 records, either every page needs custom archaeology, or the layering degenerates into an ambient texture that means nothing. Ambient layering that means nothing is precisely the outcome the audit condemned.

**Risks.**
- **"Faint = false."** The most serious epistemic hazard in this document. It directly contradicts "not established is not false."
- **Legibility and accessibility.** Overlapping text and low-contrast strata are hard to make WCAG-viable, hard to give keyboard access to, and hard to express in a linear fallback.
- **Antiquarianism.** Palimpsests, ghosts, and residue slide easily into nostalgia and craft-fetish — the opposite of a contemporary knowledge environment.
- **Mush.** The default failure is a beautiful, unreadable, meaningless grey.
- **False history.** Layering implies chronology; not every distinction is chronological, and implying that one thing superseded another when it merely differs from it is an academic error, not a visual one.

---

### Hypothesis C — The Critical Apparatus

**One sentence.** *Academic Concept Lab behaves visually like a critical edition: a stable, beautifully set text carrying the scholarship, and a live, fully attributable apparatus that intervenes on it.*

**World model.** Two spatial registers that never merge. A measured typeset column holds the claims. Around and between it — margin, gutter, interlinear space — lives an apparatus of interventions, each of which is *attached to a specific piece of text* and *attributed to a specific voice*. The characteristic sight is **a precise mark reaching into a precise phrase**, with a sigil saying who is speaking. The register is scholarly, quiet, exacting, and dense with responsibility rather than with colour.

**Spatial logic.** Structure comes from **attachment**: where an intervention hooks into the text, and how many interventions cluster on one passage. A heavily contested claim visibly carries more apparatus than a settled one — which is an honest, emergent density, not an asserted one. Intellectual structure affects composition through *the distribution of intervention*.

**Typography / drawing relationship.** The sharpest and cleanest separation of the three. Typeset text is *what is claimed*. Drawn marks are *who is claiming it and with what standing*. Handwriting never carries primary content — which is exactly the brief's requirement that handwriting be intervention rather than the site's default voice.

**Material behaviour.**
- *Mark type* carries claim class, in a declared notation extending the existing `●` `■` `▲` `✦` `?` vocabulary from typographic sigla into drawn behaviour.
- *A firm, closed underline* = source-grounded; *a broken one* = proposed; *a wavering one* = contested. Directionality matters: an intervention that reaches from margin into text is doing something different from one written between the lines.
- *Pressure* = strength of the intervention itself (a scope warning presses harder than a cross-reference), never strength of evidence.
- *Erasure* = a retraction, attributable and dated.
- Colour = voice, secondary to mark type, never load-bearing alone.

**Theory differentiation.** The weakest area, and this must be stated plainly. Theories differ only in *where the apparatus clusters* and *what kind of intervention dominates*: Gestalt attracts competing bracketings of the same passage; Tonal attracts substitution notes; Predictive Processing attracts conditional annotations; ITPRA attracts parallel timing lines. That is real differentiation, but it is a differentiation of texture, not of structure — and the brief explicitly warns against theories receiving the same layout with different marks in it.

**Quiet scholarship.** Outstanding, and by a wide margin. The critical-edition tradition *is* quiet scholarship: sigla, apparatus, attribution, and stated editorial intervention are a thousand-year-old solution to precisely this project's problem. Evidence, sources, provenance, scope and relations all become first-class apparatus rather than trailing sections. Relation types (complements / bridges / formalises / informs / contrasts) are apparatus entries with direction, which is exactly what the contract demands.

**Annotation philosophy.** The whole interface is annotation, so the discipline must be severe: an intervention is earned only if it is *attributable* and *attached*. An unattributed or floating mark is forbidden by definition. This is the most rigorous annotation philosophy of the three.

**Interaction philosophy.** One outstanding idea: **filter the apparatus by voice.** Show only what is source-grounded; show only what is a teaching construction; show only what is unresolved. That is genuinely epistemic, impossible in print, and directly serves the acceptance test — a reader can ask "which of these statements is Concept Lab's own arrangement?" and see the answer. This may be the single best interaction idea in this document.

**Long-page rhythm.** Naturally good. Passages with little apparatus read fast and clean; contested passages slow the reader down through visible density. The rhythm is produced by the scholarship itself rather than by design decisions, which is the most trustworthy kind.

**Digital-native quality.** Apparatus filtering; apparatus that can point at a phrase without a footnote round-trip; interventions that can be traced to their source record. All impossible on paper, all epistemic.

**Scalability.** Best of the three mechanically — the apparatus is generic, and every record gets one for free. But this is also its failure mode: 100 records would look identical, which converts a strength into the sameness problem the brief rejects.

**Risks.**
- **Conservatism.** This is the most likely of the three to end up as "a well-set editorial website with pencil marks added" — a named prohibition.
- **Weak identity.** It could be mistaken for a very good digital scholarly edition rather than something only Academic Concept Lab could be.
- **Margin collapse.** The two-register spatial idea is a desktop luxury; at narrow widths the apparatus must relocate, and if it relocates into the flow it becomes footnotes.
- **Apparatus overgrowth.** With nine claim classes, a rich page could carry more marks than text.
- **Deference.** Critical editions treat the text as sacred. Here, much of the "text" is Concept Lab's own synthesis, and the design must not lend it borrowed authority.

### What the stress tests actually show

**Three findings, and they were not the expected ones.**

1. **Hypothesis A fails precisely where the knowledge is simultaneous rather than conditional.** Gestalt's irreducible ambiguity, Predictive Processing's recurrence, and ITPRA's Prediction ∥ Reaction parallelism all involve things being true *at the same time*, and a comparison of conditions structurally implies *instead of*. This is not a polish problem; it is a category limit. A needs a simultaneity device.
2. **Hypothesis B is excellent at exactly two things** — irreducible coexistence, and intellectual history that must not collapse — **and actively harmful at most others**, especially anything flat and precise (evidence X-rays), anything about weighting rather than time (precision), and anything where implying chronology would be an academic error.
3. **Hypothesis C is the only one that passes the quiet scholarship acceptance test by construction**, because its default state is text with attribution, and no control needs operating for the eight questions to be answerable. A's central risk is that its meaning lives in a comparison the reader has not yet made.

Read together, these do not point to a winner. They point to a *division of labour*: A owns the page's organising question, C owns the register of scholarship and attribution, and B owns one specific device that A cannot supply — **holding two states present at once without implying that one replaced the other.**

| Scholarly credibility | High — controls are visible | **Lowest risk-adjusted** — layering can look like assertion | **Highest** |

### Reading the comparison

Hypothesis B is the most dramatic and it should not be chosen. It scores lowest on accessibility, scalability, and — decisively — on scholarly safety: its core material convention (pressure = standing) is one careless reading away from asserting that faint means false, which the contract explicitly forbids. It is a device, not a world.

Hypothesis C is the safest and it should not be chosen alone. It scores well almost everywhere and *worst on the one criterion the brief treats as the whole point*: theory-specific composition. A system where all four records receive the same two-register layout with different marginalia is the named failure mode. It is a register, not a world.

Hypothesis A is the only one whose organising idea comes from the knowledge itself rather than from a visual tradition, and the only one that produces genuinely different page shapes for genuinely different geometries. Its two weaknesses — simultaneity, and the acceptance test — are exactly the strengths of the other two, and they are addressable without dilution.

## Part 6 — Recommendation

**Recommendation: option B in the brief's terms — a deliberate synthesis of Hypothesis A and Hypothesis C, plus exactly one bounded device borrowed from Hypothesis B.**

Working name: **Conditions and Apparatus.**

> Academic Concept Lab is a page where something is held still while its conditions change, set in a scholarly register where every mark is attached to a claim and attributed to a voice.

### What each contributes

**From A — the organising principle of the page (dominant).**
- The primitive is *condition*, not force.
- Every conceptual passage is a controlled question: a declared invariant, a declared variable, a declared boundary.
- The number and kind of a theory's conditions determine its page geometry. This is the mechanism by which theories become structurally different.
- P1 holds absolutely: no mark without a difference.

**From C — the register of scholarship and the attribution system (co-dominant, but subordinate in the page's structure).**
- Two registers that never merge: typeset claim, drawn intervention.
- Every mark is *attached* to a specific claim and *attributed* to a claim class. There are no floating marks.
- The existing `●` `■` `▲` `✦` `?` vocabulary extends from typographic sigla into drawn behaviour, with each convention declared locally and given a text equivalent.
- Apparatus filtering by voice is adopted as the platform's signature non-comparative interaction.
- Quiet scholarship is solved here, in C's register, not in A's.

**From B — one device only: the retained trace.**

Adopted under a strict contract:

1. It applies **only while a comparison is live** — when the reader changes a condition, the prior state may remain present at reduced pressure so the change itself is inspectable.
2. It applies **only where the knowledge is about coexistence or revision**: Gestalt's irreducible ambiguity, Tonal Hierarchy's dynamic updating, Predictive Processing's recurrent update, ITPRA's appraisal revising without erasing, and the Gestalt intellectual-history stages.
3. It is **always declared in text** at the point of use, including what it does not mean.
4. Reduced pressure means **superseded or prior**, never *weaker*, *less true*, or *discredited* — and the local declaration must say so.
5. It is **never ambient**. There is no layered background, no sedimented ground, no decorative ghosting anywhere on the site.

### What must remain separate

This is the part that makes the synthesis a design rather than a blend:

- **B's ground is rejected entirely.** Only the device is taken. If a surface anywhere reads as a palimpsest at rest, the synthesis has failed.
- **C's margin is a register, not a layout.** The two-register idea must survive at narrow widths as *attachment and attribution*, not as a physical margin. If the apparatus degenerates into footnotes on mobile, C has been misapplied.
- **A's comparison must never be manufactured.** Where a record's knowledge contains no genuine invariant and variable, it gets no comparison. Such a record is set in C's register alone — which is also the answer to A's scalability caveat, and the site's second mode.
- **Quiet scholarship is not a quieter version of A.** It is C. Evidence, Scope, Limitations, Provenance, Sources and Relations are apparatus, set flat, precise and typeset, and they must not acquire hatching, fields, or conditions to look consistent with the loud sections. The rhythm the brief asks for comes from *changing register*, not from dimming one.
- **A's registration discipline is absolute.** The invariant is mechanically identical across conditions. A hand-varied constant is a lie about constancy.

### The material contract, restated for the synthesis

| Material operation | Proposed meaning | Must never be read as |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Held mark, mechanically identical across conditions | a deliberately held invariant | that its *meaning* is unchanged |
| Re-hatched field around the held mark | the changed condition | strength, effect size, or salience |
| Accumulated hatching / repeated passes | amount of investigation; convergent sources | probability, confidence, or correctness |
| Broken or partial boundary | provisional, defeasible, or scope-limited | missing or incomplete content |
| Retained construction lines | a teaching construction (`▲`) or editorial arrangement (`✦`) | a source-authored figure |
| Retained trace at reduced pressure | a prior or superseded state, kept inspectable | falsified, weak, or obsolete |
| Two hatch directions coexisting | two readings genuinely held open | a conflict the denser one has won |
| Declared frame with no mark inside | an omission — predicted but absent | nothing / an error |
| Erasure with visible residue | an attributable revision by the platform | deletion or retraction of the source |
| Colour | voice / claim class | strength, quality, or correctness |

Every row is a **hypothesis to be tested**, not an established convention, and every row must be declared in text wherever used, per P3. The middle column is a proposal; the right-hand column is a requirement of the freeze.

### Why not simply A

Because A alone fails the quiet scholarship acceptance test and cannot express simultaneity, and because A alone would slowly convert every record into a comparison — including records whose knowledge has no invariant. C's register is what keeps A honest, and B's single device is what keeps A from implying "instead of" where the knowledge says "as well as."

## Part 7 — First prototype plan

**Not to be implemented in this phase.** This is the proposed next experiment.

The prototypes below are chosen to **falsify** the recommendation, not to flatter it. Each targets a named weakness rather than a strength, and each is a *section*, not a page — the object is to test behaviour, not to produce a beautiful screen.

### The four prototypes

**1 · Notation specimen — "Conditions and Apparatus, declared."**
The material contract from Part 6 rendered as a single working page: each convention shown, declared, given its text equivalent, and given its explicit non-inference. Not a swatch sheet — every specimen must be shown doing real work on real record content.
*Tests:* whether the notation reads epistemically rather than decoratively; whether P2 survives contact with a real reader's instinct that dark means strong; whether the conventions are distinguishable at all without colour.

**2 · Tonal Hierarchy — "same note, different home", section only.**
The most favourable case for the recommendation, included as the control. One physical C4 held mechanically constant across four declared contexts, with the three levels — judgment, profile, interpretation — kept in visibly separate registers, and no exact profile values anywhere.
*Tests:* whether registration is perceptible enough to carry the argument; whether the three-level separation survives; whether "no measured chart" is a limitation or a liberation.

**3 · ITPRA — the outcome hinge, section only. The adversarial case.**
Deliberately the record the stress tests marked worst for Hypothesis A. Requires the outcome hinge, Prediction ∥ Reaction as genuine simultaneity, appraisal revising without erasing, and the four concurrent expectation sources — all without a five-stage timeline and without implying an algorithm.
*Tests:* whether the synthesis can express simultaneity at all; whether C's parallel attachment and B's retained trace do the work the stress tests predicted. **If this prototype fails, the recommendation is wrong and Hypothesis C should be reconsidered as the dominant partner.** This is the decisive experiment.

**4 · One quiet scholarship environment.**
For a single record: evidence X-rays with design / tested / found / notTested intact, scope and stopping points, qualifications, sources with contributions, typed relations with direction, and provenance. Set entirely in C's register.
*Tests:* whether quiet reads as *rigorous and calm* rather than *plain*; whether the acceptance test's eight questions are answerable with nothing operated; whether the register change from prototype 2 or 3 into this one produces the rhythm the brief asks for, or just a drop in quality.

Gestalt and Predictive Processing are **deliberately excluded** from the first round. Both are flattering to the recommendation — cue conditions and precision conditions are almost too easy — and neither would tell us anything the first four do not. Gestalt's intellectual-history stages should enter round two, since they test the retained trace against an academic-error risk rather than a legibility risk.

### Conditions on the prototypes

- Isolated experiment routes only. No production route, component, or stylesheet is touched, and nothing is deployed.
- Real record content from the frozen handoff. No lorem, no invented findings, no invented numerical values — and specifically no exact probe-tone profile values, which the record forbids until direct source verification.
- Every constructed element labelled `▲` with its manipulated variable, its held constants, and its non-inference boundary stated.
- Static/linear fallback, keyboard equivalence, visible focus, reduced-motion behaviour, and non-colour-only provenance built in from the first commit, not retrofitted. These are semantic requirements here, not accessibility chores: the contract says a distinction that exists only in an interaction does not exist.
- Both light-condition legibility and contrast checked for every material convention, since pressure and hatching are exactly where contrast requirements bite.

### Falsification criteria — what would sink this recommendation

- Readers consistently interpret denser or darker marks as stronger evidence, despite declaration. → P2 is unachievable; the material system needs rebuilding around a non-intensity variable.
- The ITPRA prototype cannot express Prediction ∥ Reaction without implying sequence. → A is the wrong dominant partner.
- The quiet environment reads as the boring part rather than the calm part. → the register change is not carrying rhythm, and the synthesis has a hole where the brief's central problem is.
- The notation cannot be distinguished without colour, or cannot be stated in text. → it fails the contract outright.
- Prototypes 2 and 3 look substantially the same. → theory-specific composition has not been achieved, and the recommendation has collapsed into C.

### Stop rule

If two or more falsification criteria trigger, return to Part 6 rather than iterating on the visuals. The failure would be in the hypothesis, not in the execution.
